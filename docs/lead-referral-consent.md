# Lead referral consent: template wording

Plain-language wording for the moment a firm asks a client's permission to pass their request to another independent firm, when the receiving firm pays a flat fee for the introduction. Written for a small professional-services firm (engineering, architecture, inspection) that cannot take every request it receives and would rather hand it to a peer than drop it.

Not legal advice. Have your own attorney review it against your state's professional rules, consumer-protection law and privacy law before use, and keep a dated version number on the wording so you can prove what a client agreed to.

## Principles the wording follows

- Nothing is shared until the client presses a button. A referral is never implied by submitting the original request.
- The client sees exactly what will be shared, item by item, including each file by name.
- The receiving firm is named. If several firms may receive it, all are named, and the wording says the request goes to whichever picks it up.
- The fee is disclosed in the same screen, in one sentence, before the button: who pays whom, that it is flat, and that it does not change the client's price or oblige them to hire anyone.
- The wording says the referring firm may offer the request to another named-or-equivalent firm if the first declines, so a second offer does not need a second email.
- The consent is single-use: one button press, one answer, recorded with the wording version, the firms named, the items shared, the time, and the client's IP and browser string.
- The client can say no in one tap, with no consequence beyond the referring firm not making the introduction.

## Consent screen

> **Share your request with {Firm A}?**
>
> {Firm A} is an independent {profession} firm that may be able to help. If you confirm, we will share:
>
> - your request (what you told us about the project)
> - your name, email, phone and the property address
> - {N} files you sent: {file names}
>
> Their team can then reach out to you directly. If {Firm A} can't take it on, {Referring Firm} may offer the same request, on these same terms, to another independent {profession} firm. Nothing is shared until you press the button.
>
> **[ Yes, share it with {Firm A} ]**   [ No thanks ]
>
> A quick note for transparency: {Firm A} may pay {Referring Firm} a small, flat fee for sharing your request, whether or not you decide to work together. This doesn't change what you pay, and you're under no obligation to hire them. {Firm A} operates independently and is responsible for its own proposals and {profession} services.
>
> Consent wording {VERSION}.

When several firms may receive the request, replace "{Firm A}" in the heading and button with "{Firm A}, {Firm B} or {Firm C}", "Their team" with "Whichever firm picks it up", and the fee sentence with "{Firm A}, {Firm B} or {Firm C} may pay …" and "Each firm operates independently …".

## After-the-fact disclosure (for the receiving firm's first message, or your own follow-up)

> A quick note for transparency: {Firm A} is an independent {profession} firm and may compensate {Referring Firm} with a small, flat fee for sharing your request. This does not affect your pricing or obligate you to use their services. Any {profession} work is performed independently by {Firm A}.

## What to record

One row per consent, kept with the request: wording version, firms named, items shared (as the client saw them), timestamp, IP, user agent, the answer (yes / no), and which firm ultimately took it. That row is what you produce if a client, a regulator or a licensing board ever asks.

## Versioning

Give the wording an id and date (for example `LC-1.1 (2026-11-03)`) and print it at the bottom of the screen. Any change to the words the client agrees to is a new version; the record keeps the old one.
